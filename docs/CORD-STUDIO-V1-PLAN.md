In the pilot, supported time is 4/4. Keep user key, meter and tempo authoritative. In a fresh session choose a playable 4/4 default at 104 BPM. Select across compatible progression lengths, with an explicit reason for each excluded row. Start with a record's native mode when building a factory fixture, or a verified mapping under the player's selected mode. Retain alterations, quality-defining intervals and slash-bass targets when transposing. Diatonic seeds can resolve in a supported selected mode when their degrees and resulting voicings pass validation. Skip a sourced record if its mode or explicit voicing cannot safely map. The full catalogue stays available through the existing browser. Include an eight-chord 4/4 fixture such as Canon to exercise the timeline, even if its style weight is low.

## Reference techniques

JIANT confirms the value of seeded player streams, phrase-level bass shapes, resolving approaches and composing against a kick map. PERF-1 makes the sounding result available while its controls are in view. Tessera shows how per-track behavior and development at longer timescales can coexist. CORD can combine these principles with its own four parts, rhythms, pitch vocabulary and control bindings. [JIANT generator](https://github.com/juanjiant-bit/JIANT/blob/main/firmware/src/gen.c) · [PERF-1 quick guide](https://github.com/deamonSLINKY/PERF-1/blob/main/docs/QUICK.md) · [Tessera](https://github.com/Rastapopolix/tessera)

## Architecture

- **Shared song context.** Carry the stable progression ID, resolved roots and masks, explicit bass targets, voiced pitches, chord durations, meter, tempo and absolute transport tick. Cache the current and next chord for composition. Reuse the existing key/modifier priorities. An intentional New Take can change the plan at a short musical boundary without resetting the absolute tick or MIDI clock. Publish that plan and all dependent phrase versions as one transaction. A locked or muted player keeps its settings and mute state. Any phrase carried into new harmony must be revoiced or replaced against the new plan before it can sound.
- **Four player contexts.** Each holds a playing style within the pilot profile, activity, variation, mute, level, seed and current phrase. Separate phrase RNGs from arrangement and sound decisions. The phrase builder takes a read-only song context and writes a sorted, bounded list of onsets, velocities and durations with per-part voice caps. The long-running state is a short recognizable motif plus small mutations at phrase boundaries. In an automatic development cycle, revisit one or two weak events and preserve the groove and motif. Measure real variation over several minutes.
- **Kick and Bass.** The band establishes stable kick anchors per groove. Bass can use the committed kick accents for doubles, replies and held notes. A Drum-only change respects those anchors while varying hats, secondary kicks and fills. It keeps the committed Bass phrase intact. A Bass-only change reads the drum snapshot and never advances the Drum RNG. This makes the dependency directional at composition time and avoids regeneration cascades.
- **Foreground publication.** Use fixed-capacity active and prepared buffers per part. Compose and validate outside the callback. Publish a slot and revision in a short IRQ-protected handoff. Track a harmony revision, per-part request revision and buffer ownership so obsolete requests cannot resurrect notes after Stop, a key change or a newer request. Commit a part at the next safe scheduler opportunity, aligned to the already-running phase. Retain matching common tones where safe, release only the replaced generated notes, and start eligible new events. Never replay earlier transients from the new phrase. Allow a still-active sustained event to begin with its remaining duration if it fits the current harmony. Test identical non-target event streams against the same seeded control run.
- **One clock and explicit note ownership.** Take ticks from `cpa_tick1()` and service the existing event/output block. Reuse the lower-level `sqp_*`, `cfp_*` and drum lane helpers where their release semantics fit. Adapt pause, clock loss, stop and handover to legacy playback to release all four generated owners. Include same-pitch manual/automatic overlap and Bass mono takeover. Suppress a generated retrigger on a held manual voice, and let manual playing take priority on a contested physical part. Layer ARP uses part 2 and the existing Layer owner or explicit prepared Layer notes. Its note-offs cannot clear Chords. A Studio mute gates generated events and releases generated notes. Instrument level is a separate live control and applies to the addressed synth part, including manual playing on it.
- **Fast controls.** Acknowledge the control on the next UI update and publish level, mute and Space at the next audio control block, with a short existing smoothing path where applicable. For style, density and New Part, aim to prepare and hand off within one sixteenth-note interval at pilot tempos when there is a playable onset, about 144 ms at 104 BPM. Measure request → prepared → commit → first changed audible onset separately. Show the pending boundary if it slips. A change during a scored rest can commit immediately and become audible at the next intended note. Handle key/tempo edits through existing transport policy. Patch browsing uses the existing staged path, with part-scoped safe handoff and a held-note deferral when a patch change would cut a manual voice. Keep optional beat/chord quantization and Back One out of the initial listening build.

## First listening build

One Shoegaze / Dream Pop profile, with two playing styles per instrument and independent activity. Use existing patches and kits. Keep one mix baseline, retain the player's edits and avoid automatic sound or FX changes during a paired listening test.

| Player | First musical vocabulary |
| --- | --- |
| Chords | **Sustain** and **Soft pulse**. Start on bar one with inversions that minimize inner-voice motion. Carry common tones between chords and preserve the source's defining quality. Favor a three- or four-voice register above Bass with headroom for guitar. Keep attack and pulse accents under the guitar. Flag a voicing that exceeds the measured cap instead of silently changing its quality. |
| Bass | **Hook** and **Counterline**. Start with four original, transposable families. A low anchor followed by a high two-note answer, a descending guide-tone line with a late pickup, a held note across the backbeat followed by an offbeat reply, and a two-bar call with a deliberately empty response beat. Each family specifies accents, durations, rests and pitch functions. Resolve chord-tone targets from the actual mask, anchor slash chords on their written bass, and aim pickups at the *next* chord. Constrain range and leaps, repeat the identifiable first gesture, and vary a pickup, answer, or weak subdivision every few bars. Select the first four by listening, then expand to at least ten archetypes once the strongest shapes are clear. |
| Drums | **Soft backbeat** and **Open hats**. Create two original kick/snare/hat grammars, with consistent backbeat, a small accent range and sparse phrase-end fills. A stable kick anchor lets the Bass choose where to double and where to leave space. Use existing kit voices and lanes. |
| Layer | **Arp** and **Sparse motif**. Use one soft, initially monophonic instrument above Chords. Arp follows voiced chord tones. Sparse motif leaves whole beats empty and develops one small contour over several repetitions. Low default activity gives the guitarist room. |

Use a temporary Jam surface. PLAY starts a prepared band from a normal first run. Use existing clockwise K4 New Take at overview and New Part in focus, scoped to the selected musician. Coalesce fast encoder turns. ALGORITHM selects one of four musicians in the pilot surface, and SCL/SEL enters or leaves focus there. In focus, K1 chooses playing style, K2 activity, K3 level, and one counterclockwise K4 gesture toggles the focused part’s mute. Label both K4 directions in focus. Keep overview K3 Space. Put the applied/pending result on the current screen. These are pilot bindings to test on the panel. Bare OCT+/OCT− stay octave controls, including the existing combined panic/boot gestures. Preserve legacy bindings in their own pages. PLAY follows the existing Stop, recording and playback arbitration.

### Deliverables and acceptance

1. Package a versioned M1 `.fwsc`, checksum, control card and known-good rollback reference. Re-link and record XIP/RAM/POOL and executable identity before hardware review. Smoke test on the device with manual keys, stop/start, USB streaming and each part muted or regenerated. Exercise saved patches, Songs, legacy REC and backup round trips using test data.
2. Provide ten fixed-seed stereo examples and ten matched Bass-only regenerations, plus event traces as supporting evidence. Freeze harmony, drum plan, Layer, patches, mix, tempo and the exact comparison window for each pair. Record seed, source ID, BPM and generation revisions. Include host stereo renders for repeatability and real FM-1 captures for the listening review. Use a short long-form take to hear development and one paired take with guitar played over it. Compare at matched output level.
3. On hardware, PLAY must produce a useful band from bar one, Chords must sustain while Layer moves, and all four parts must regenerate without a transport reset. Compare untouched tracks to seeded controls and exercise same-note overlaps, repeated requests, muted regeneration and manual playing while stopped. Require no hangs, stuck notes, hard clicks, repeatable clipping, USB discontinuities or overload shedding in the pilot stress matrix.
4. Listen for a recognizable bass hook, a useful contrast between replacements, a pocket with the kick and room to play guitar. Target at least eight useful riffs out of ten matched pairs in the user's review. The full UI phase depends on this listening decision.

## UI design review for the later pass

The HTML demonstrates the four tabs, zoom, shared focus while switching tabs, part-specific control editing and mute state surviving New Take. Its Back action clears selection, the meters are level indicators, and the heading key/tempo is hardcoded even as chord sets change. Recording and several Help/mix actions are placeholders. Keep separate state for **focused musician** and **zoom depth**, and drive the chord, position and activity readouts from engine telemetry.

| Workspace | Overview → detail |
| --- | --- |
| Studio | Transport, chord and four musician tiles → selected part's style, activity, variation and New Part. |
| Sound | Four patch/kit rows → sound, level, tone and available FX controls. Existing deep editors remain reachable. |
| Recording | Four aligned lanes → existing lane editor. Sequence and Songs remain accessible from this workspace. |
| Help | Contextual topic list → current workspace or musician's help. Returning restores focus. |

At 240 × 240, keep a compact status band and control/value readout anchored. Recreate the **Bass Something 2 camera move** in CORD's own graphics. The inspected FM-1 animation pans and scales the same instrument panel toward the edited control, shows its live value and reverses back to the whole panel, with the status chrome fixed. Apply that geometry from the selected musician tile to its detail view. A transient knob close-up can return to its origin on idle. Deliberate musician detail stays open until Back or HOME. New input updates the value during the move and can interrupt or reverse it. Motion Off snaps directly to the destination and disables mascot movement. Reuse CORD's rendering primitives and existing Cat/Devil assets. Budget a few interpolated frames in the existing viewport and measure foreground/LCD time before considering additional storage or surfaces. [Bass Something 2 reference](https://imijes.github.io/bass-something-2/)

For the eventual shell, use ALGORITHM to move the same focus in Studio, Sound and Recording. HOME returns to Studio overview, SELECT/SCL navigate locally, and PRESETS acts on the named workspace target. Reuse dedicated sound/SEQ buttons as entry points after checking conflicts. A held GLO overlay should consume only deliberately mapped shortcuts and restore the prior view when released. A GLO tap keeps access to existing settings. Prototype this with MIDI input, simultaneous held keys and octave gestures before rebinding. The current panel routes HOME and ALGORITHM through HOME banks, GLO through settings, SEQ through Accompaniment/Sequencer and REC through legacy take handling. Those routes need explicit scope guards.

## Hardware risks

| Risk | What to verify |
| --- | --- |
| Link margin | Verified G1.1 link uses 566,112 of 573,440 XIP bytes and 91,184 of 98,304 ordinary RAM bytes. Pool use is 316,792 of 344,064. The first two margins are only 7,328 and 7,120 bytes. Use bounded packed events, budget both active and prepared buffers, replace G1-only generator logic where possible, and compare ELF maps. Measure stack separately. |
| Shared voices and note-off | Three pitched parts plus drums use engine-dependent voice limits. Generated chords, Layer, long FX tails and held manual notes compete. Start with a modest polyphony limit, low Layer activity and no new DSP. Log steals, kills, deadline counters and CPU, with USB on/off and on supported single- and dual-core paths. Check held MIDI notes, pedal state and release ordering on every change. |
| Patch and kit handoff | The existing staged loader can clear MIDI/pedal state, call all-off for a part and use one ranked tail. Rapid patches can outrun it. Reuse its single-part preparation where safe. Defer when necessary, show pending and leave patch swaps out of phrase regeneration. |
| Foreground stalls | Measure the first PLAY, input-to-sound latency and clock continuity under drawing and USB load at 44.1/48 kHz. Compose an initial idea once the saved settings are known. Cache candidates off the audio ISR. Confirm a rapid burst can coalesce without starvation or a stale-buffer race. |
| Storage and recovery | Keep existing layout, slot IDs and object versions. Avoid automatic flash writes from regeneration. Exercise prior saves and A/B recovery with a fixture backup. Preserve safe boot, calibration, panic and update routes. |
